Commercial readiness review

Assessment date: 16 October 2026. Author: Michele Giletto. This is an internal product decision record, not a claim that a customer has accepted the software.

Decision

Orbital has a defensible purpose as a configurable technical training and procedure exploration application. A public engineering showcase and a tightly scoped customer evaluation can be justified after the technical release gate passes. A claim of operational mission assurance, institutional deployment readiness, proven commercial demand or validated educational benefit is not supported by the present evidence.

The new mission capabilities should be demonstrated as one coherent workflow: choose a customer collection, modify a scenario, execute docking or recovery with bounded propellant, inspect the assessment, replay the run and export the delivery. Deorbiting should demonstrate the intended burn mode and a descending interface event without implying ground impact prediction. This is more useful to a prospective buyer than a long feature list.

Primary evidence

ESA's September 2026 Space Environment Report, based on activity through 2025, describes growth in orbital traffic and the continued need for disposal and debris prevention. It establishes the relevance of lifecycle operations. It does not measure the market for training software. [ESA Space Environment Report 2026](https://www.esa.int/Space_Safety/Space_Debris/ESA_Space_Environment_Report_2026)

ESA's commercialisation material identifies debris removal, life extension and servicing as developing service categories. This supports selecting those exercises. Its aggregate revenue projection covers in space services and must not be presented as Orbital's addressable market. [ESA in space services](https://commercialisation.esa.int/industries/in-space-services/)

The ESA Phoenix contract announcement of 23 September 2026 describes development of a commercial GEO servicing spacecraft and docking interface. This is evidence of active industrial development in the domain, not an endorsement, customer relationship or claim that Orbital satisfies that programme's requirements. [ESA Phoenix announcement](https://resilience.esa.int/archives/news/esa-selects-clearspace-to-advance-recurring-geostationary-orbit-servicing-through-phoenix-partnership-project)

ESA's RISE mission material describes a rendezvous and docking demonstration intended to support life extension. It reinforces the operational relevance of proximity and lifecycle exercises. It does not validate this software. [ESA RISE mission material](https://www.esa.int/ESA_Multimedia/Videos/2025/07/RISE_ESA_s_mission_extender_in_geostationary_orbit/%28lang%29/en)

Likely initial buyer and task

The most plausible first buyer is a technical training provider, an engineering teaching group or a small spacecraft team that needs a configurable exercise and a repeatable review. This is an inference to test. The user of the software is an instructor or engineer; the buyer may be a training lead or programme manager. Institutional procurement teams usually need security and deployment evidence beyond what a public prototype demonstrates.

The initial offer should be a paid or unpaid evaluation with one agreed scenario and a short defined schedule. Deliver the scenario definition, runnable application, model assumptions, test record and final review. The acceptance conversation must settle the required physics fidelity, scenario confidentiality, installation environment and support responsibilities before price or delivery commitments are made.

Concrete value hypotheses

The editor may reduce the work required to prepare variants of a repeat exercise. Replay may reduce the effort needed to reconstruct an operator's choices. A source bound result may improve traceability when an exercise definition changes. Local assets and a controlled delivery may reduce integration friction in a restricted network. These remain hypotheses until measured in the buyer's workflow.

Measure preparation time, corrections needed before the exercise, failed or incomplete runs, time to reconstruct a run, missing evidence in the final review and instructor usefulness ratings. Compare the same task with the customer's existing process. Do not publish a percentage benefit without an agreed baseline, enough observations and the customer's permission.

What can be sold after evaluation

Potential commercial scopes are adaptation of scenario collections, a customer deployment with agreed support, integration with the customer's identity and record systems, and maintenance of approved exercise content. A license to the platform must define permitted users and sites, redistribution, updates, support, data handling and responsibility for validation. Pricing has not been validated. Invented subscription tiers would obscure that uncertainty.

Competitive position

The proposed differentiation is the combination of authoring, execution, assessment and controlled delivery in one local application. This review did not perform an exhaustive competitor comparison or establish uniqueness. Existing orbital analysis and training tools must be evaluated against the buyer's actual task before claiming replacement, superior accuracy or reduced cost.

Public source and proprietary rights

The current LICENSE preserves proprietary ownership and references EVALUATION.md. That document grants 30 days of internal nonoperational evaluation, including running the application and creating local scenarios. It requires no registration, signature or activation service. Redistribution, paid instruction, production use and commercial deployment are excluded. This makes a buyer trial possible without mislabeling the code as open source. Any longer or commercial use needs further permission.

GitHub's public repository terms allow viewing and forking through the service even when no additional software license is granted. Removing public visibility later does not retract already acquired copies. A public showcase repository containing documentation, screenshots and selected evidence can therefore be commercially safer than exposing all implementation details. If the complete source is published, do so deliberately, preserve third party notices and state the rights without overstating exclusivity. [GitHub repository licensing](https://docs.github.com/en/repositories/managing-your-repositorys-settings-and-features/customizing-your-repository/licensing-a-repository)

Release gate

The exact final revision must pass the full numerical, mission, authorization, persistence, replay, library, package and browser checks. Independently review any finding that could relabel a run, change ownership or bind an assessment to the wrong scenario. Document unresolved limitations plainly. Inspect a clean extracted delivery. Ensure all visible copy matches the code and excludes invented customer outcomes. Freeze a version and compute hashes only after these checks.

Market evidence is not a substitute for this gate. Passing tests is also not proof of a market. After release, the next business milestone is a qualified evaluation with an identifiable workflow and an agreed measure of usefulness.

Current unvalidated points

Willingness to pay, adoption, customer references, learning improvements, preparation savings, target hardware performance, formal accessibility, independent physical validation, production resilience and institution specific security accreditation remain unvalidated. A public release may accurately show engineering work and invite evaluation. It must not present these unmeasured outcomes as completed results.
