The library is available from the Mission Simulation Hub. It contains twelve executable definitions across orbital transfer, docking, recovery, deorbiting, inspection and separation. Definitions include client, revision, initial state, budget, tolerance and three competencies. Search filters name, description and family. Selecting a mission previews it; Open mission starts the appropriate executor.

Editor parameters are consumed by the engine. Validation rejects nonfinite values, missing client references, duplicate identifiers, HTML and transfers incompatible with budget or propulsion limits. Three learning objectives name the evidence associated with the selected family's checks. Editing text does not change that family's assessment formula. Each save increments revision. Removal retains previous versions for recovery.

Clients creates dedicated collections, assigns independent template copies, edits workspace name and accent, and generates deliveries. Import merges clients and adds missing or newer mission revisions. Restoring a version creates a fresh revision. Connected saves persist on the API with optimistic concurrency; stale writes are rejected. Without an API edits remain on the device. Client collections within one tenant are editorial groupings. The security boundary between organizations is the OIDC tenant, not the client field alone.

Docking, passive recovery and deorbiting launch in the advanced inertial engine. The optional advanced scenario is stored with the mission definition and travels with library import, export and client delivery. The server resolves the selected mission identifier and revision from the verified tenant library before starting a run. It preserves that definition with the run, so a later edit cannot change the meaning of an earlier assessment.

Configure advanced scenario exposes epoch, the central gravity or J2 model, engineering drag, wet and dry mass, specific impulse, drag area and coefficient, orbit orientation and anomaly. Proximity settings include radial and cross track offsets, rotating relative velocity, ordered hold distances, hold tolerance, corridor angle, capture speed and alignment limits, passive target rotation and bounded scalar control authority. Deorbit settings include perigee target and minimum descent rate. The entry boundary is fixed at 120 km. Initial altitude and inclination, along track range, capture distance and speed, budget and disposal perigee must remain consistent with the main mission fields.

Scenario validation rejects unknown fields, nonfinite vectors, repeated spacecraft identifiers, invalid mass relationships, unordered holds, inconsistent main parameters, invalid capture envelopes and unsupported entry boundaries. One to eight holds must decrease toward the target and the final hold must fit inside the capture envelope. Three text objectives remain required. Their labels describe the selected family's numerical checks; editing a label does not replace its assessment formula.

Docking requires ordered holds, a clear approach corridor, bounded relative speed and explicit capture after all instantaneous conditions pass. A capture after a corridor deviation remains a captured with deviations result. Recovery additionally requires roll alignment and relative roll rate for the synchronization objective. Its controller is scalar, without rigid body contact or target detumbling. Deorbiting uses impulse or finite thrust, mass accounting and a localized descending 120 km event. It does not compute atmospheric trajectory, heating, impact point or ground dispersion.

Inspection and separation retain the legacy linear Clohessy Wiltshire educational model in a local circular orbit frame. Inspection requires three positions and three explicit stationary acquisitions. Separation requires at least one kilometer or five times initial distance, whichever is greater, within budget. These executors are not general nonlinear rendezvous solvers and must not be conflated with advanced docking or recovery.

Mission assessments lists advanced runs, including incomplete and aborted attempts, with their original client, revision, measured score and signed receipt. Individual review restores the scenario and ordered journal. Legacy result records remain available for the older executors. With the API, grades come from the authorized authoritative session. Supplied labels cannot assign a completed run to another mission or client. Legacy CSV text fields neutralize spreadsheet formula prefixes.
