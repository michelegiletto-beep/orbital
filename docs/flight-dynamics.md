Orbital 0.3 is a Mission Simulation Hub with a dark English operating workspace. Mission control, Flight dynamics, Mission library, Mission designer, Client workspaces and After action are working modules. Docking, passive recovery and deorbiting execute in the advanced inertial engine. Transfer training and the educational inspection and separation exercises retain their disclosed legacy executors.

The advanced workspace supports eight spacecraft and 32 maneuvers within a seven day horizon. State uses Earth centered inertial Cartesian coordinates with Z pointing north, kilometers, seconds and kilograms. Input delta V is in meters per second. Vehicle creation specifies perigee altitude, eccentricity, inclination, ascending node, true anomaly, wet mass, dry mass, specific impulse, drag area and coefficient. Argument of perigee is currently fixed to zero.

The gravity model uses μ = 398600.4418 km³/s², equatorial reference radius 6378.137 km and J2 = 0.00108262668. Altitude uses a spherical mean reference radius of 6371 km. Central gravity and the conventional axisymmetric J2 acceleration are integrated by fourth order Runge Kutta with a configurable maximum step from 0.5 to 10 seconds. Start times, cutoff times and propellant depletion split integration steps. The generic flight workspace stops a vehicle after a step reaches the 120 km interface. The advanced deorbit mission instead localizes the descending crossing within the step and stops the shared mission at that event. Entry heating, atmospheric flight, fragmentation and landing are excluded.

Atmospheric drag uses a static representative log density table from 120 to 1000 km, a spherical co rotating atmosphere and constant drag coefficient and area. Density above 1000 km is set to zero. It is an engineering sensitivity model, not a complete implementation of a standard atmosphere or a space weather service. Solar activity, winds and density uncertainty are not modeled.

Impulsive burns apply a velocity change along velocity, opposite velocity, radial or orbit normal directions. Mass follows the rocket equation. Finite burns apply thrust along the selected instantaneous direction, integrate mass flow using thrust divided by specific impulse and standard gravity, and cut thrust at dry mass. Burn guidance follows the instantaneous direction without actuator or attitude lag. Overlapping maneuvers on one spacecraft are rejected. Different spacecraft can burn simultaneously.

Forecasts propagate the actual selected force model and planned maneuvers. Solid orbit curves in the 3D view are instantaneous osculating two body curves; dashed curves are numerical forecasts. The ground view projects numerically propagated states onto geocentric latitude and longitude. Greenwich sidereal angle uses UTC as an approximation of UT1. Earth orientation parameters, precession, nutation and polar motion are excluded. Rendering illumination is illustrative. Spacecraft markers are enlarged for visibility.

The dispersion tool runs 8 to 64 seeded trajectories with independent Gaussian position and velocity error on each ECI axis. The seed, input standard deviations, horizon and sample count are recorded with the result. The displayed median, 95th percentile and maximum are empirical position errors relative to a nominal propagated trajectory. They are not collision probabilities or formal confidence guarantees. Spacecraft properties and commanded thrust are not sampled. Forecast closest approach is a sampled pairwise separation at 60 second intervals, not an operational conjunction assessment or continuous minimum distance solver.

A flight archive stores canonical initial elements and the exact command journal. Browser restoration and server checkpoint creation both reconstruct the initial state and replay all commands. Modified initial vectors, invalid plans, excessive journal length and excessive computation are rejected. Archives accept at most 2000 commands and 400000 spacecraft integration step estimates per replay. Browser history is bounded by deterministic decimation. The API signs the digest of the archive, computed workspace and authorization resource in the audit chain. Checkpoint retrieval verifies the receipt and replays again. The settings module lists authorized checkpoints and restores them. This verifies reproducibility within this implementation; it does not establish physical certification or independent validation.

Advanced docking and recovery propagate the chaser and passive target independently in ECI. Measurements use the target radial, along track and orbit normal frame. Initial rotating relative velocity is converted to inertial velocity with the frame rotation contribution, and measured relative velocity removes that contribution. Gravitational and drag acceleration are evaluated at the actual inertial states. The guidance controller applies bounded finite inertial thrust, accounts for propellant through mass flow and follows ordered hold points. It is an ideal controller with direct state access; navigation and actuator uncertainty are not simulated.

Capture requires all configured holds, approach from the negative along track side inside the corridor, distance between collision radius and capture radius, relative speed within its bound, roll alignment and roll rate within their bounds. Contact is a terminal logical event. Segment proximity checks detect a crossing between integration samples, but no mechanical contact dynamics are solved. Docking also requires an approach without corridor excursions for full achievement. A later capture can therefore produce a captured with deviations result. Recovery uses a passive target rotation and a bounded scalar roll tracker. It does not torque or stabilize the target body.

Advanced deorbit starts from a circular chaser orbit. An impulsive maneuver computes the retrograde velocity reduction for the requested perigee. A finite maneuver applies retrograde thrust and stops at the requested osculating perigee or the available resource boundary. Actual expenditure, elapsed burn time and resulting perigee are computed. Completion requires a deorbit command, perigee below 120 km and arrival at the descending 120 km interface with the scenario minimum descent rate. The requested lower perigee is a maneuver target, not a prediction of atmospheric trajectory or impact location.

An advanced mission snapshot contains the client mission revision, force model, both vehicle specifications, relative initial conditions, hold points, corridor, capture conditions, scalar attitude parameters, budget, disposal target and three objective labels. Mission duration is bounded to 24 hours and the journal to 2000 commands. Ordered sequence and exact precommand simulation time are validated during replay. The API signs the authoritative snapshot, resource, journal and recomputed assessment. Client collection membership is an editorial attribute within an authenticated tenant.

Automatic tests compare a circular orbit against analytical closure and energy conservation, J2 nodal drift against the first order secular rate, finite burn fuel consumption against the rocket equation, dry mass cutoff, drag energy loss, integration step convergence, boundary timing, archive replay, invalid inputs and seeded uncertainty reproducibility. Advanced mission tests exercise every included docking, recovery and deorbit variant, custom vehicle and capture settings, unsafe capture rejection, budget exhaustion, collision, abort, finite disposal and localized entry. API tests cover signed persistence, ownership metadata, stale command sequences, altered files, rollback and restoration after restart. Exact release outcomes belong in docs/verification.md. Cross platform bit identity and independent flight dynamics benchmark campaigns remain unverified.

Reference material: [NASA orbital dynamics modeling](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/20120007107/downloads/20120007107.pdf), [US Standard Atmosphere 1976](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/api/citations/19770009539/downloads/19770009539.pdf), [IERS conventions](https://www.iers.org/iers/en/dataproducts/conventions/conventions), [IERS Earth orientation data](https://data.iers.org/bulletins.php). These references inform model assumptions; their citation does not imply that this implementation reproduces every convention or standard.

The software is an engineering training environment. Third body gravity, higher gravity harmonics, radiation pressure, covariance propagation, validated collision probability, terrain, full rigid body attitude, mission communications, sensor simulation and actual vehicle commanding are outside the implemented model. Use requires independent validation before any operational flight decision.
